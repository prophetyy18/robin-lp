# T033 — Handle confirmations and reorgs

> Phase: `P03`

## Dependencies

T032

## Outcome

T033 owns the finality policy and reorg handling for Robinhood
Chain / Ethereum-compatible JSON-RPC providers used by the P03
ingestion pipeline. It enforces a single, explicit, configurable
finality policy whose formal boundary is the RPC `finalized` tag,
and it pairs that policy with deterministic common-ancestor search,
orphan marking/replacement, deep-reorg halt, reorg journal, and an
append-only critical-incident recorder so that canonical views
converge while audit history remains intact.

- canonical views converge while audit history remains intact;
- T033 = confirmations / reorg handling with a finalized-tag policy;
  the formal finality boundary is the JSON-RPC `finalized` tag
  returned by the qualified endpoint (R9). The configurable finality
  policy must use that tag as the formal boundary;
- unfinalized data (the window between `finalized + 1` and `latest`)
  may be fetched and observed for liveness, but it is **never**
  promoted into the qualified dataset;
- finalized raw evidence is append-only and is never rewritten in
  place; a declared critical incident may authorize creation of a
  superseding canonical generation, but never in-place mutation of
  the original finalized raw evidence.

## Deliverables

### Configurable finality policy

- a **configurable finality policy** whose formal boundary is the
  RPC `finalized` tag (R9). The policy is consumed by the storage
  and ingestion layers; the policy object exposes, at minimum, the
  finality tag name, the qualified endpoints that must agree on the
  finalized boundary, the timeout / retry semantics for the
  finalized-tag request, and the escalation path when the
  finalized tag is unavailable, falls back, errors, or disagrees
  between qualified endpoints;
- the unfinalized hash window is defined as **`finalized + 1` to
  `latest`**. Data inside this window may be fetched and observed,
  but it must not enter the formal qualified dataset;
- the finality policy is **explicitly injected** into every test and
  into every component that decides finality. Code under test must
  not depend on any implicit production default;
- **fail-closed** behaviour: when the finalized tag is unavailable,
  returns an error, times out, falls back, or two qualified endpoints
  disagree on the finalized block hash or ancestry, qualification
  halts and the system must not silently continue with a degraded
  finality assumption;
- no automatic fallback to a fixed confirmations count is permitted.
  `confirmations = 12` is retained **only** as an
  `EXPERIMENTAL_NOT_LIVE_APPROVED` placeholder for legacy
  configuration values and historical fixture references. It must
  not be used as a basis for mainnet qualification, paper trading,
  or live promotion;
- reason codes emitted by the finality policy layer include, at
  minimum: `finalized_unavailable`, `finalized_ancestry_disagreement`,
  `finality_tag_unavailable`, and `FINALIZED_ANCESTRY_VIOLATION`.

### Common-ancestor search and orphan marking/replacement

- a **common-ancestor search** that, given the current canonical
  chain tip and an observed chain head from a qualified provider,
  locates the deepest block whose hash is shared by both views and
  is reachable as a parent chain through the stored canonical
  history;
- **orphan marking / replacement** that demotes blocks above the
  common ancestor to orphan status, records the demotion reason in
  the reorg journal, and re-evaluates the affected canonical rows
  against the new fork without deleting the orphaned raw evidence;
- orphan raw logs are retained, not deleted. Reorg demotion marks
  rows as orphan and records the reason; the original bytes remain
  auditable.

### Deep-reorg halt

- a **deep-reorg halt** that triggers when the orphan depth
  exceeds the policy's deep-reorg threshold. The halt stops new
  backtest qualification, paper trading decisions, and live
  decisions, records an append-only critical-incident candidate,
  preserves every existing raw partition, every existing canonical
  generation, and every provider evidence record, and escalates to
  the Phase 8 operator / incident workflow for human declaration
  and recovery authorisation.

### Reorg journal

- a **reorg journal** (append-only, checksummed alongside the
  manifest per ADR-002) that records each orphan block, its
  replacement block, the common ancestor that anchored the reorg, the
  policy in effect at the time of the reorg, the reason code
  (`shallow_reorg`, `deep_reorg`, `provider_disagreement`,
  `removed_log`, `orphan_reappearance`, etc.), and the linkage from
  old raw partitions to the new observations. The reorg journal
  never replaces or rewrites finalized raw evidence.

### Critical-incident candidate recorder (append-only)

- an **append-only critical-incident candidate recorder** that
  captures the detection event for `FINALIZED_ANCESTRY_VIOLATION`
  (see Acceptance) without overwriting any prior incident record
  and without rewriting any finalized raw evidence. The recorder
  surfaces the candidate to the Phase 8 operator / incident
  workflow and blocks new backtest qualification, paper, and live
  decisions until a human incident declaration and re-checking have
  taken place.

## Acceptance

- **shallow / deep reorg fixtures converge or halt as specified**:
  shallow reorgs below the configured threshold resolve through
  common-ancestor search and orphan marking/replacement; deep
  reorgs above the configured threshold trigger the deep-reorg
  halt, persist the critical-incident candidate, and block new
  qualification / paper / live decisions;
- **provider disagreement fixture halts qualification**: when two
  qualified endpoints disagree on the finalized block hash or
  finalized ancestry, qualification stops and the system fails
  closed with reason code `finalized_ancestry_disagreement`;
- **removed-log fixture**: a log that was once observed and later
  marked `removed` is demoted via orphan marking with a recorded
  reason; the original raw evidence is preserved;
- **orphan-reappearance fixture**: an orphan block that reappears on
  a new observation is reconciled against the reorg journal
  without rewriting the prior raw evidence, and the resolution is
  recorded as a new append-only evidence entry;
- **finalized data is never rewritten without a critical incident**:
  finalized raw evidence is append-only; a critical incident
  authorizes creation of a superseding canonical generation after
  operator review, but does not authorize overwriting, deleting,
  or in-place rewriting the original finalized raw evidence;
- **tests explicitly inject the finality policy**: every test that
  exercises finality or reorg behaviour passes an explicit finality
  policy; no test depends on an implicit production default;
- **`confirmations = 12` is an `EXPERIMENTAL_NOT_LIVE_APPROVED`
  placeholder only**: fixture tests may reference it, but mainnet
  qualification, paper trading promotion, and live promotion must
  never depend on it;
- **fail-closed on finalized unavailability, fallback, error, or
  ancestry disagreement**: when the finalized tag is unavailable,
  returns a fallback value, errors, or two qualified endpoints
  disagree on the finalized block hash / ancestry, qualification
  stops and the system fails closed. There is no automatic
  fallback to a fixed confirmations count;
- **`FINALIZED_ANCESTRY_VIOLATION` is detected, halted, journaled,
  and escalated**: when a block previously marked finalized under
  the then-effective policy later falls out of the finalized
  ancestry, or when qualified endpoints persistently disagree on
  finalized block hash / ancestry, the system:
  - halts immediately;
  - records an append-only critical-incident candidate;
  - blocks new backtest qualification, paper, and live decisions;
  - preserves every old raw partition, every old canonical
    generation, and every provider evidence record;
  - saves the new fork / observation as new append-only evidence
    rather than overwriting or deleting the prior finalized raw
    evidence;
  - escalates to the Phase 8 operator / incident workflow;
  - allows creation of a superseding canonical generation **only**
    after a human incident declaration and re-checking have taken
    place;
- **finalized raw evidence is never rewritten, including during a
  critical incident**. A declared critical incident may authorize
  creation of a superseding canonical generation after operator
  review;
- **reason codes are emitted for every failure path**:
  `finalized_unavailable`, `finalized_ancestry_disagreement`,
  `finality_tag_unavailable`, and `FINALIZED_ANCESTRY_VIOLATION`
  must all be surfaced as distinct reason codes the manifest can
  record and the operator can triage.

## Must not

- equate confirmations with finality across chains. The formal
  finality boundary is the RPC `finalized` tag, not a confirmations
  count;
- delete orphan raw logs. Reorg demotion marks rows as orphan and
  records the reason; the bytes remain auditable;
- continue decisions during unresolved ancestry. Unresolved
  common-ancestor search, unresolved finalized-ancestry
  disagreement, or any pending critical-incident candidate blocks
  new qualification / paper / live decisions;
- overwrite, delete, or in-place rewrite finalized raw evidence at
  any time, including during a critical incident. A superseding
  canonical generation is permitted only after operator review and
  only as a new append-only generation;
- automatically fall back to a fixed confirmations count when the
  finalized tag is unavailable. The system fails closed;
- use `confirmations = 12` as a basis for mainnet qualification,
  paper trading promotion, or live promotion. It is retained only
  as an `EXPERIMENTAL_NOT_LIVE_APPROVED` placeholder for legacy
  configuration and fixture references;
- perform Phase 8 operator / incident handling inside T033. T033
  detects, emits the reason code, halts, journals the candidate,
  and escalates; the human incident declaration, re-checking, and
  recovery approval are owned by Phase 8;
- weaken any Acceptance clause in order to make a partial
  implementation pass. A failing acceptance clause is a contract
  gap, not a test bug.
