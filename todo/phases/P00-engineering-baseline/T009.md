# T009 — Enforce testable acceptance criteria in every task contract

> Phase: `P00`
> Contract status: `OWNER_PLAN_EXTENSION_2026-09-19`

## Dependencies

T006

## Outcome

a contract that cannot decide its own task — an Acceptance section that states no checkable
condition, or that leaves the rule its Deliverables states to be enforced by nobody — fails a
machine check instead of reaching a Developer, so the confusion an amendment has to repair is
caught when the contract is written.

## Deliverables

1. An offline, deterministic checker (a `tests/` module plus any helper under `tools/`), parsed
   from the contract sources with the standard library and never imported or executed, covering
   every contract `todo/phases/**/T[0-9]{3}.md`, that fails on:
   - a missing or empty Acceptance section, or one that states no falsifiable condition: no
     declared verification surface (a test path or module, a command, a fixture, golden, vector,
     manifest, report, ledger, checksum or schema), no explicit pass or fail statement, and no
     rejection, refusal or invalid-input condition;
   - a declared placeholder marker in the Acceptance section — an unresolved decision such as
     `TBD`, `TODO`, `to be determined`, `as appropriate` or `if possible` — because a criterion
     that defers itself is not a criterion;
   - a quantitative rule stated in the Deliverables section that Acceptance never requires: a
     comparison against a quantity carrying a unit or a percent (a relation such as `<=`, `>=`,
     `<`, `>`, `at least`, `at most`, `above`, `below`, `more than`, `less than` or `exceeds`)
     must appear in the Acceptance section as a required condition on the same quantity with the
     same direction. The two already-confirmed economic rules that reached the contracts through
     Deliverables alone — the complete 5-minute target-token USDG return `<= -80%`, and the
     complete 5-minute return above 100% — are what this rule exists to catch before review
     instead of after it.
2. The declared closed vocabulary of deliverable 1 — the verification surfaces, the placeholder
   markers and the comparison and unit forms — is committed beside the check so a reviewer can
   re-derive every finding, and the finding report names the contract, the section, the line and
   the matched text.
3. A closed exception list in `docs/implement/ci/suppressions.toml`, the registry and the `rule`,
   `reason`, `owner` and `expiry` fields T006 delivers, reused rather than reinvented: an entry
   that no longer matches a finding is itself fatal, a failure without an entry is fatal, and the
   finding list is printed with the exception list so a reviewer sees every deviation and its
   expiry.
4. The check wired into CI as a required check, without weakening an existing job.

## Acceptance

the check passes on the committed repository and runs as a required CI check; each of the three
rule families has a seeded regression that fails the check, committed beside it, and the
quantitative-rule regression includes a contract text that states a threshold in Deliverables which
its Acceptance never requires — the two historical cases above are the grounded ones — because a
check that never fires is indistinguishable from one that passes
(`tests/test_documentation_links.py` is the pattern, including its explicit can-it-fail test); the
check reports, for every finding, the section, the line and the text it matched, and the declared
vocabulary is printed with them so a reviewer can reproduce the finding by hand; an empty contract
set, a contract with no Acceptance section, an unreadable or unparsable exception entry and an
empty scan each fail closed instead of being skipped or defaulted; each entry of the exception list
carries a reason, an owner and an expiry, and a stale entry is fatal.

## Must not

narrow a rule, empty or shorten the declared vocabulary, or move a case out of it to make the
check pass; accept an Acceptance section because it is long, because another contract in the same
phase passes, or because a reviewer might read a condition into it; skip, xfail or otherwise step
past a finding; edit a contract to silence one — a finding in a contract this task may not write is
recorded as an exception with `reason`, `owner` and `expiry`, or reported and routed when it is a
specification defect; add a third-party dependency or a network read, or import or execute a
project module at check time; treat the check as a substitute for review — it is a floor against
untestable criteria, not evidence that any task is complete; weaken an existing CI job, test or
assertion; edit Intent, Spec, `tools/workflow/`, `.claude/`, `todo/schemas/` or `todo/config.yaml`.
